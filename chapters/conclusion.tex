This thesis investigated whether neuroevolution can produce cost-to-go heuristic functions
for A* search on Boxoban that are substantially more parameter-efficient than a
conventional gradient-trained CNN baseline. Four heuristics were implemented and compared
on 294 unseen levels under an identical 100{,}000 expansion budget: a parameter-free
Manhattan-distance heuristic that anchored the minimum baseline, a fixed convolutional
network with 2 million parameters adapted from DeepCubeA that anchored the upper-limit
baseline, a NEAT-evolved feed-forward network, and a CoDeepNEAT hybrid that combined
evolutionary architecture search with gradient-descent weight training.

The Manhattan-distance heuristic, summing per-box minimum distance to a goal,
solved 87.4\% (257/294) of the held-out levels with zero trainable parameters. This is
the floor that any learned heuristic must beat to justify its training cost. The CNN
baseline solved 100\% (294/294) under the same budget and serves as the upper-limit
reference. NEAT evolved a heuristic of just 25 parameters (4 internal nodes and
21 enabled connections, roughly 0.001\% of the CNN baseline's parameter count) and
achieved an 84.0\% solve rate, falling 3.4 percentage points \emph{below} the
parameter-free Manhattan baseline. This shortfall is consistent with NEAT's known
weakness on high-dimensional inputs: weight-perturbation search across a $500$-binary
input grid cannot recover enough gradient information to outperform a purely
geometric estimate. CoDeepNEAT, which coevolves dense-dropout modules and blueprint
topologies and trains each assembled genome by gradient descent, discovered a
$500 \to 320 \to 320 \to 1$ architecture with 263{,}361 parameters (roughly
$7.7\times$ smaller than the CNN baseline) and achieved an 88.8\% solve rate.
CoDeepNEAT is therefore the only neuroevolved method in this thesis that
\emph{beats} the Manhattan-distance baseline; the addition of Adam gradient descent
on each assembled genome is what closes the gap that pure NEAT cannot.

These results sharpen the case for hybrid neuroevolution. Pure topology-and-weight
evolution from scratch is not enough on inputs of this dimensionality, so it loses
to a parameter-free analytical heuristic. Pairing evolutionary architecture search
with gradient-based weight optimization recovers enough accuracy to clear the
analytical baseline while still operating at a fraction of the gradient-trained
upper bound. The gap between CoDeepNEAT and the CNN (11.2 percentage points)
remains non-trivial, but the scale of the parameter reduction relative to the
modest drop in solve rate, and the fact that CoDeepNEAT clears the Manhattan floor
that pure NEAT cannot, suggests that hybrid neuroevolution is a viable strategy
for heuristic compression when pure topology evolution is too constrained but a
fully hand-designed CNN is too parameter-heavy.

\subsection{Future Work}

The empirical separation between pure NEAT and CoDeepNEAT, on opposite sides of the
Manhattan baseline, indicates that further investigation should concentrate on the
hybrid method rather than on additional scaling of pure topology-and-weight evolution.

Based on the NEAT topology figure, one can deduce that weight perturbation can not supply the granular updates trequired by a $500$-dimensional binary input and this is unlikely solved by larger population or longer evolutionary runs alone. The more
productive intervention is to replace the weight optimiser, as CoDeepNEAT does.
Future work should therefore prioritise CoDeepNEAT-style hybrids in which an
evolutionary outer loop searches over architecture while a gradient-based inner loop
performs weight tuning, allowing architecture search to proceed without being confounded
by stochastic weight perturbation.

Two extensions follow directly. First, the gradient-descent inner loop in CoDeepNEAT
trains each assembled genome on a fixed subsample for a fixed number of epochs.
Systematic ablation of this inner loop, varying the subsample size, optimiser, loss
function, number of epochs, and fitness-aggregation rule across blueprint assemblies,
would clarify which components of the gradient signal are responsible for the
performance gain over pure NEAT. The same outer-evolution, inner-gradient pattern can
also be applied to plain NEAT genomes by interposing a short gradient-descent
fine-tuning pass within each fitness evaluation, providing a controlled test of whether
gradient information alone is sufficient to raise pure NEAT above the Manhattan
baseline, independent of the module/blueprint coevolution machinery.

Second, the upper-limit baseline in this thesis is a convolutional network, and its
100\% solve rate constitutes the strongest evidence presented here that convolutional
inductive bias underlies the recovery of a generalised cost-to-go estimator on
Boxoban's $10 \times 10$ grid. The dense-dropout module space searched by CoDeepNEAT
does not provide this prior, which offers a plausible account of why CoDeepNEAT
plateaued at 88.8\%. Extending CoDeepNEAT's module space to include convolutional
blocks parameterised by filter count, kernel size, stride, and optional pooling, and
correspondingly extending NEAT's primitives to admit shared-weight convolutional
connections in place of purely dense ones, would bring together the three ingredients
that distinguish the upper-limit baseline from the neuroevolved methods: spatial
inductive bias, gradient-based weight optimisation, and parameter-efficient architecture
search. Closing the remaining 11.2-point gap to the CNN while preserving the parameter
advantage already demonstrated by CoDeepNEAT is the most plausible role for this
convolutional-hybrid direction in subsequent work.
