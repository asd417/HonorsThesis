This thesis investigated whether neuroevolution can produce cost-to-go heuristic functions
for Weighted A* search on Boxoban that are substantially more parameter-efficient than a
conventional gradient-trained CNN baseline. Three approaches were implemented and compared
on 294 unseen levels: a fixed convolutional network with 2 million parameters, a
NEAT-evolved feed-forward network, and a CoDeepNEAT hybrid that combines evolutionary
architecture search with gradient-descent weight training.

The CNN baseline established a strong reference point, achieving a 100\% solve rate
on held-out levels within the 100{,}000 node expansion budget. NEAT produced a heuristic
of just 24 parameters: a 99.9988\% reduction in parameter count compared to the CNN
baseline while achieving an 84.0\% solve rate, a striking result considering the
extreme reduction in model complexity. CoDeepNEAT, which coevolves dense-dropout modules
and blueprint topologies and trains each assembled genome by gradient descent, discovered
architectures in the 32{,}000 to 170{,}000 parameter range (between 12$\times$ and 63$\times$
smaller than the CNN baseline) and achieved an 88.8\% solve rate, narrowing the gap to the
CNN by combining evolutionary architecture search with gradient-based weight optimisation.

Both neuroevolved heuristics are meaningfully weaker than the CNN baseline, but the scale
of the parameter reduction relative to the modest drop in solve rate suggests that
neuroevolution is still a viable strategy for heuristic compression. The relative
performance order also suggests that the inclusion of
gradient-based weight training within an evolutionary architecture search is a
productive middle ground when pure topology evolution is too constrained but a
fully hand-designed CNN is too parameter-heavy.

\subsection{Future Work}

Several directions merit further investigation. Longer NEAT runs with larger populations
may find topologies with greater expressive capacity without abandoning the parameter
efficiency that makes the approach attractive. CoDeepNEAT could be extended to search
over convolutional module types rather than dense-dropout blocks, which may better suit
the spatial structure of the Sokoban grid. One may also investigate applying convolutional layers to NEAT for potentially competitive results.

The question of whether a neuroevolved heuristic function can match the performance of a CNN baseline heuristic remains an important question. An ideal discovery would be to find a neuroevolution method that can produce a complex heuristic function with less trainable weights than the CNN baseline, while maintaining a competitive solve rate.
