This thesis asked whether neuroevolution can produce cost-to-go heuristic functions
for A* search on Boxoban that are substantially more parameter-efficient than a
conventional gradient-trained CNN baseline. The four-way comparison reported in
Chapter~\ref{ch:results} answers in the affirmative, but only conditionally: pure
topology-and-weight evolution from scratch is not enough on inputs of this
dimensionality, while pairing evolutionary architecture search with gradient-based
weight optimization recovers enough accuracy to clear the analytical baseline at a
fraction of the gradient-trained upper bound.

The split between NEAT and CoDeepNEAT is the load-bearing finding. NEAT's failure
to clear the Manhattan floor, despite searching over both topology and weights,
is consistent with its known weakness on high-dimensional inputs: weight
perturbation cannot supply the gradient information required to outperform a
purely geometric estimate on a $500$-binary input grid. CoDeepNEAT keeps the
evolutionary architecture search but replaces NEAT's weight perturbation with
Adam gradient descent on each assembled genome, and that one change closes the
gap. The
implication is that the productive locus of neuroevolution research on inputs of
this dimensionality is the hybrid: evolution chooses the architecture, gradients
tune the weights.

The remaining gap between CoDeepNEAT and the CNN is non-trivial, but the
parameter reduction relative to the modest drop in solve rate, and the fact that
CoDeepNEAT clears the floor that pure NEAT cannot, suggests that hybrid
neuroevolution is a viable strategy for heuristic compression when pure topology
evolution is too constrained and a fully hand-designed CNN is too parameter-heavy.

\subsection{Future Work}

The empirical separation between pure NEAT and CoDeepNEAT, on opposite sides of the
Manhattan baseline, indicates that further investigation should concentrate on the
hybrid method rather than on additional scaling of pure topology-and-weight evolution.

Based on the NEAT topology figure, one can deduce that weight perturbation cannot supply the granular updates required by a $500$-dimensional binary input, and this is unlikely to be solved by a larger population or longer evolutionary runs alone. The more
productive intervention is to replace the weight optimizer, as CoDeepNEAT does.
Future work should therefore prioritize CoDeepNEAT-style hybrids in which an
evolutionary outer loop searches over architecture while a gradient-based inner loop
performs weight tuning, allowing architecture search to proceed without being confounded
by stochastic weight perturbation.

Two extensions follow directly. First, the gradient-descent inner loop in CoDeepNEAT
trains each assembled genome on a fixed subsample for a fixed number of epochs.
Systematic ablation of this inner loop, varying the subsample size, optimizer, loss
function, number of epochs, and fitness-aggregation rule across blueprint assemblies,
would clarify which components of the gradient signal are responsible for the
performance gain over pure NEAT. The same outer-evolution, inner-gradient pattern can
also be applied to plain NEAT genomes by interposing a short gradient-descent
fine-tuning pass within each fitness evaluation, providing a controlled test of whether
gradient information alone is sufficient to raise pure NEAT above the Manhattan
baseline, independent of the module/blueprint coevolution machinery.

Second, the upper-limit baseline in this thesis is a convolutional network, and its
100\% solve rate constitutes the strongest evidence presented here that convolutional
inductive bias underlies the recovery of a generalized cost-to-go estimator on
Boxoban's $10 \times 10$ grid. The dense-dropout module space searched by CoDeepNEAT
does not provide this prior, which offers a plausible account of why CoDeepNEAT
plateaued. Extending CoDeepNEAT's module space to include convolutional
blocks parameterized by filter count, kernel size, stride, and optional pooling, and
correspondingly extending NEAT's primitives to admit shared-weight convolutional
connections in place of purely dense ones, would bring together the three ingredients
that distinguish the upper-limit baseline from the neuroevolved methods: spatial
inductive bias, gradient-based weight optimization, and parameter-efficient architecture
search. Closing the remaining gap to the CNN while preserving the parameter
advantage already demonstrated by CoDeepNEAT is the most plausible role for this
convolutional-hybrid direction in subsequent work.
