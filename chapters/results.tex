\section{Experiments and Results}

All three heuristics were evaluated on 294 held-out levels from the Boxoban medium set
under Weighted A* search with weight $w = 1.0$ and a maximum node expansion budget of
100,000. The CNN baseline was additionally evaluated across a range of training set sizes
to characterise its data scaling behaviour.

\subsection{CNN Baseline}

The CNN baseline is trained on 14{,}000 BFS-solved puzzles drawn from the Boxoban
medium split and evaluated on a disjoint set of 294 puzzles from the same split. With
this training set, the CNN achieves a 100\% solve rate (294/294) under the 100{,}000
node expansion budget. The 97.3\% figure that appears in the data-scaling analysis is
not a separate baseline but a single point from an earlier ablation that varied the
training set size: at the 14{,}000-level training point in that ablation the CNN solved
286/294, while subsequent runs with refined training (matching the configuration used
elsewhere in this thesis) reach 100\%. Both numbers are measured at the same 100{,}000
expansion budget on the same held-out 294 puzzles.

The data-scaling curve (Figure~\ref{fig:cnn_scaling_solve}) shows that solve rate
rises steeply with training set size and then plateaus: 69.7\% at 100 levels,
94.9\% at 1{,}000 levels, 97.6\% at 5{,}000 levels, and saturates beyond
that. This plateau is the empirical justification for using a 14{,}000-level training
set rather than the full nine-million-level Boxoban corpus: the heuristic is not
data-limited beyond a few thousand training examples, and additional levels yield
diminishing returns at substantial BFS-solving cost.

The 100{,}000 expansion budget is imposed deliberately so that the CNN, NEAT, and
CoDeepNEAT heuristics are
compared under identical search-time budgets. A less accurate heuristic will explore
more nodes per level to reach a solution, and without a fixed cap the comparison
would conflate heuristic quality with total search cost. The 100{,}000 budget was
chosen to be generous enough that the CNN rarely hits it --- the 2.7\% of unsolved
levels reflect the tail of the expansion distribution, not a failure of the learned
heuristic --- while being tight enough that the smaller neuroevolved heuristics are
meaningfully constrained. Reporting all three methods under the same cap therefore
isolates how heuristic accuracy translates into search efficiency.

\subsection{Interpretation of Findings}

The results confirm that neuroevolution can produce heuristics that are radically more
parameter-efficient while remaining useful for A* search. NEAT converges to a genome
with 7 nodes and 17 connections --- 24 trainable parameters in total, representing
less than 0.002\% of the CNN baseline's parameter count. CoDeepNEAT discovers
architectures in the range of 32,000 to 170,000 parameters, between 12$\times$ and
63$\times$ smaller than the CNN baseline. The gap in solve rate relative to the CNN is
non-trivial, but the degree to which both neuroevolved heuristics close this gap while
operating at a fraction of the parameter budget suggests that heuristic compression
through neuroevolution is a direction warranting further study.
