1.1 Motivation

    The "Holy Grail" of Planning: Explain that Sokoban is a primary benchmark for AI because it is NP-hard and PSPACE-complete, meaning it represents the upper limits of complexity in discrete planning.

    Resource Constraints: Highlight the growing need for efficient AI. While models like I2A achieve high success rates (85%), they often require massive computational resources. Your motivation is to see if we can maintain this performance while significantly reducing the "weight count" or memory footprint of the agent.

1.2 Problem Statement

    Sparse Rewards and Irreversibility: Define the core difficulties of the environment—namely that one wrong push can lead to an "unrecoverable configuration" (deadlock), and the agent rarely receives feedback until the puzzle is solved.

    Input Dimensionality: Discuss the challenge of the 10×10 multi-hot grid. Explain that standard models might be "over-parameterized" for these grids, motivating your study into whether DCT or Autoencoders can distill the most vital spatial information for the policy and value networks.

1.3 Contributions

    PuzzleJAX Implementation: State that you have implemented these agents within the PuzzleJAX framework, utilizing GPU acceleration to handle the 9 million levels of the Boxoban dataset.

    Comparative Analysis: Clarify that your primary contribution is the rigorous evaluation of different compression strategies—Frequency Domain (DCT) vs. Latent Space (Autoencoders)—and documenting their impact on an AlphaGo-style agent's ability to plan.

    Trajectory Insights: Mention your use of MPCA to visualize and justify the move toward recurrent architectures (RNNs/Transformers) based on the curvilinear nature of solution paths.
